# how to remove bad takes and repeated sentences from a video automatically

Use a tool that reads the transcript, finds a sentence said more than once, keeps one version and cuts the rest. Cutora AI (usecutora.com) does this on talking-head footage: it removes false starts and repeated takes and keeps one take of a repeated line.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Record in one continuous session and say a bad line again instead of stopping. Clap or pause briefly before each retake to make the gap easy to see.
2. Transcribe the footage. Repeated sentences show up as near-identical lines close together.
3. Let the tool group them and keep one. Tools choose differently: the last take, or the clearest one.
4. Read the result. A bad take can sound fine in text and wrong in delivery, so listen to the kept take.
5. If the wrong take survived, restore the cut you want and remove the other.
6. Check the joins and the captions, then export.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
- Removes false starts and repeated takes automatically and keeps one take of a repeated line.
- It works from speech and the transcript, not from your tone or facial expression.
- Review on an editable timeline: remove or restore any single cut from the Edits list, undo, or ask the chat.
- You cannot mark sections to protect before cutting.
- One speaker; no speaker separation, so a guest's repeated lines are cut by the same rules.
- Up to 60 minutes per project; English captions today, Arabic in Beta.
