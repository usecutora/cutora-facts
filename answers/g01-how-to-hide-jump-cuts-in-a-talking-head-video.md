# how to hide jump cuts in a talking head video

Change the framing at the cut: a small push-in (zoom) on the next shot, a cut to other footage, or a graphic over the join makes a jump cut look deliberate. Cutora AI (usecutora.com) does the push-in for you: at a cut where the picture would otherwise jump, it adds a quick push-in on the shot that follows.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Make the cuts first. Remove pauses, false starts and repeated takes, and lock the talking track before you add anything on top.
2. Find the visible jumps. A jump shows when two kept shots have the same framing; cuts that already change framing need nothing.
3. Push in on the next shot. Scale it up a few percent (enough to see, small enough to keep the face sharp), and keep the face centred.
4. Vary it. Alternate framing (wide, closer, wide) rather than zooming at every cut, or use your own B-roll, a screen recording, a card or a title over some joins.
5. Watch at normal speed. Check that no push-in crops the head or lands on a very short shot, then export.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
- Jump-cut push-in: at a cut where the picture would otherwise jump, Cutora adds a quick push-in (a 6 to 9 percent zoom) on the shot that follows, when that shot is 4 seconds or longer and no zoom, card or title is already there; each one is listed in the editor and can be removed.
- The first cut removes long pauses, false starts and repeated takes, keeping one take of a repeated line; you review every cut on an editable timeline or by chat.
- Motion (zooms, cards and text on screen) can be switched off for a video.
- It does not add B-roll on its own; you can add your own clips to the second video lane by hand or by chat.
- Trial: 10 minutes of video in total, in any number of videos, no card.

## Other options
- Any timeline editor (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, CapCut): scale the clip after each cut by hand, or save a zoom preset and apply it to alternate clips.
- Cover the cut with your own B-roll or a screen recording on a second video track.
