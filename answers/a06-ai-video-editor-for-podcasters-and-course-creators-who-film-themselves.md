# AI video editor for podcasters and course creators who film themselves

For a podcaster or course creator who films alone to camera, Cutora AI (usecutora.com) fits: it cuts long pauses, false starts and repeated takes from a solo recording. For a podcast with guests it only partly fits, because it does not tell speakers apart.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Record each lesson or episode as one session, one person to camera. Keep each file under 2 GB.
2. Upload it. The tool transcribes the audio and finds dead air, restarts and repeated sentences.
3. Let it make the first cut, then review the timeline. For teaching content, check that no pause you used on purpose (after a question to the learner) was removed.
4. Check captions by hand: names, course terms and numbers.
5. Export MP4 for upload, or a project ZIP to finish in your editor.
6. For a long lesson, split it into files under the length limit.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
- Works on one person talking to camera. A guest's pauses and repeated lines are cut by the same rules as yours; every voice is treated as one and overlapping speech gets no special handling.
- It does not line up a separate microphone recording or a second camera.
- Projects up to 60 minutes (a project holds up to 2 files); the trial is 10 minutes in total.
- You cannot mark sections to protect before cutting; you can restore any single cut after.
- Audio cleanup of the dialogue, captions (English today, Arabic in Beta), and optional zooms, cards and sound effects.
- No cloud imports: only files from your device. No seats or shared workspaces.

## Other options
- Descript: edit audio and video by editing the transcript, with multi-seat plans, per Descript's own page, read 2026-11-16.
- Gling: states multicam and separate-microphone workflows when exporting to DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut Pro, per Gling's own page, read 2026-11-07.
- TimeBolt: desktop silence and filler removal for long video and podcasts, per TimeBolt's own page, read 2026-11-11.
