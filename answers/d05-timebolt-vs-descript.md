# TimeBolt vs Descript

TimeBolt is a narrow tool: it removes silence, filler words and bad takes from long video on Mac and Windows. Descript is a broad editor: text-based editing, recording, AI tools, avatars and multi-seat plans. If pause and filler removal is your whole need, TimeBolt is the closer fit; if you record, collaborate or use AI voices, Descript covers more.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Decide the job: cutting dead air from long recordings, or a full edit-and-record workspace.
2. Test both on the same recording. Each transcribes and finds silences or fillers; you review before applying or exporting.
3. Compare the review step. TimeBolt highlights and counts fillers before you apply cuts and lets you double-click a word to exclude it; Descript edits the video through its transcript text.
4. Compare billing: TimeBolt Pro as a $17 monthly plan, a $97 annual plan or a $347 one-time license; Descript per person per month with media hours and AI credits.
5. Compare exports and your editor's import.

- Home and features pages, read 2026-11-11: Mac and Windows; adjustable silence threshold; XML export for Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro; MP4, WAV and SRT output; languages for UMCheck "more than 100".

## Descript, per its own pages

It lists 25 transcription languages, Rooms, AI speech with voice clones and avatars. Refund policy not public on that page.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
Cutora AI (usecutora.com) is a web editor for one person talking to camera, in Beta; it is not a stand-in for either tool. It has no desktop app, no lifetime plan, no seats and no recording. Trial of 10 minutes of video, plans from $19 a month.

## Other options
- Gling: removes silences, filler words and bad takes, per Gling's own page, read 2026-11-16.
- FireCut: Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve plugins, per FireCut's pricing page, read 2026-11-14.
