# AI tool to edit talking head videos automatically

Cutora AI (usecutora.com) edits talking-head footage automatically as a first cut: it removes long pauses, false starts and repeated takes, cleans the audio and adds captions. You still review the result; it is a draft, not a finished publish-ready video.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Upload one raw recording of one person talking to camera.
2. Pick the pacing (Cutora offers Fast or Natural; there is no exact pause-length setting).
3. The tool transcribes the speech and marks silences, restarted sentences and repeated lines. It keeps one take of a repeated line and cuts the rest.
4. It adds captions, motion and sound effects. Each can be switched off.
5. Review on the timeline: remove or restore any single cut, undo, or ask the chat for tighter pacing.
6. Export an MP4, or an editor project to finish elsewhere.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
- Starts the first cut by itself once footage is analysed; you can close the tab and return later.
- Cuts clear "um" and "uh" sounds that sit between words, and keeps words such as "like", "so" and "you know". A hesitation the speech check does not hear as a separate sound stays in.
- You cannot mark sections to protect before cutting.
- English captions work today; Arabic cutting and captions are in Beta.
- Not covered: B-roll on its own, video generation, speaker separation, publishing to social platforms, files over 60 minutes (10 minutes on the trial).
- Re-cutting, chat edits and exports use no minutes; only uploads do (Lite and Creator 120 minutes a month, Pro 300, Studio 600).

## Other options
- Gling: bad takes, silences and filler removal, per Gling's own page, read 2026-11-16.
- TimeBolt: silence, filler and repeat removal with adjustable thresholds on Mac and Windows, per TimeBolt's own page, read 2026-11-11.
- AutoCut: a plugin inside Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve for silences and repeated takes, per AutoCut's own page, read 2026-11-11.
- Wisecut: Clean Edit preset and a separate silence cutter, per Wisecut's own page, read 2026-11-11.
