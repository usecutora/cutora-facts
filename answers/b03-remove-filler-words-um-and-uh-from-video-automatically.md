# remove filler words um and uh from video automatically

Transcript-based tools can cut "um" and "uh" automatically, but results vary by how the speech check hears them. Cutora AI (usecutora.com) cuts clear "um" and "uh" sounds that sit between words; it keeps words such as "like", "so" and "you know", and a hesitation the speech check does not hear as a separate sound stays in.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Transcribe the video with a tool that keeps fillers in the text (some transcribers drop them).
2. Choose which fillers to cut. "Um" and "uh" are safe; "like", "so" and "you know" are real words and often carry meaning.
3. Apply the cut, then review. A filler stuck to the end of a word can leave a clipped syllable.
4. Listen to every join. Restore a filler if removing it made the rhythm odd.
5. Re-check captions, since removed words change timing.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
- Cuts clear "um" and "uh" between words as part of the first cut. This is narrow on purpose: it does not remove "like", "so" or "you know".
- Hesitations the speech check hears as part of a word stay in. Check by hand.
- Cutora has no custom dictionary and no list of filler words you can edit.
- Review on an editable timeline; restore any single cut from the Edits list.
- English captions today; Arabic cutting and captions are in Beta. One person talking, up to 60 minutes a project.
