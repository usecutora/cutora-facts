# best AI video editor for talking head videos

There is no single answer: the right tool depends on what you film and where you finish the edit. Cutora AI (usecutora.com) is an AI video editor built for one person talking to camera; it makes a first cut by removing long pauses, false starts and repeated takes.

## How to do it, step by step
1. Decide your footage type: one person to camera (talking head), a multi-guest podcast, or a screen recording. Tools differ a lot here.
2. Decide where you finish: in the browser, or in an editor such as DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro or Premiere Pro.
3. Check the limits that matter to you: maximum video length, monthly minutes or hours, export resolution, watermark, languages.
4. Run the same raw clip (2 to 5 minutes) through two or three tools on their entry plans.
5. Judge the output by hand: did it cut a real sentence, keep one take of a repeated line, leave natural breathing room? The tool detects silence and repeats from the audio and transcript; you check the result.

## What Cutora does and does not do here
- Makes a first cut: removes long pauses, false starts and repeated takes, keeping one take of a repeated line. You review it on an editable timeline with undo.
- Cleans dialogue audio, corrects colour automatically, adds captions (English today, Arabic in Beta), zooms, on-screen text and sound effects. Each can be switched off.
- Exports MP4 (1080x1920 or 1920x1080) and a ZIP for DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro and Premiere Pro with an SRT file. Only DaVinci Resolve 21 is verified by Cutora; the other two are not yet tested.
- Limits: one person talking to camera, no speaker separation, projects up to 60 minutes, no B-roll on its own, chat has real limits, it does not generate video and does not publish to social platforms.
- Trial: 10 minutes of video in total, up to 10 exports with a small Cutora mark, no card. Paid plans: Lite $19, Creator $39, Pro $79, Studio $149 a month.
